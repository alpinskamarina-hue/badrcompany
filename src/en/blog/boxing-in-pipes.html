Short answer: a boxing (in Dutch: koof) is a small enclosure of a frame with boards around it that hides pipes, a drain, cables or an extractor duct. The frame is made of timber battens or metal stud, the boards of MDF or plasterboard. According to the Dutch platform Slimster, having one made costs € 70 to € 100 per metre on average for a simple boxing around pipes, including materials, labour and VAT. Always leave an access hatch at taps and couplings.

What is a boxing and what is it for?

A boxing is a closed casing along the wall or ceiling. It hides everything you don't want to see:

  • central heating pipes and water pipes along the wall;
  • a drain pipe coming down from above, for example in a corner of the bathroom or toilet;
  • the duct of the kitchen extractor hood;
  • cables, or lighting such as spotlights and an LED strip;
  • a curtain rail.

Sources: OPMAATZAGEN, Zelf een koof op maat maken (accessed November 2026); Slimster, Koof laten maken (5 November 2026).

For an extractor hood, Slimster recommends heat-resistant material such as MDF or gypsum fibreboard. If heavy spotlights go into a plasterboard boxing, put extra timber behind the board.

How is a boxing built up?

A boxing in a corner has two visible sides. If it runs across the middle of a ceiling or along a flat wall, it has three. Make sure the frame is firmly fixed to the wall and ceiling; the boards only hang on it.

Building a boxing in 6 steps

  1. Decide the purpose. Only pipes, or also spotlights, a curtain rail or the extractor duct? That determines the size and the material.
  2. Draw and measure. Make a sketch with all dimensions, including the thickness of the battens and boards.
  3. Have the material cut to size or cut it yourself, rather a little too long than too short.
  4. Fix the frame to the wall and ceiling with plugs and screws.
  5. Screw on the boards. Pre-drill MDF. For plasterboard, Gamma advises at most 25 cm between screws and at least 1 cm from the edge.
  6. Finish. MDF: fill the screw holes, sand, prime and lacquer. Plasterboard: joints with mesh tape, fill, then plaster or paint.

Sources: OPMAATZAGEN, Zelf een koof op maat maken; Gamma, Gyproc wand plaatsen met metal stud. How to get plasterboard smooth is explained in Finishing plasterboard.

What to watch out for

  • Access hatch. Slimster advises always fitting a hatch at couplings or taps. A 40 × 40 cm built-in hatch costs € 26.45 at Hornbach.
  • Drain noise. Rock wool or glass wool around a drain muffles the sound of running water.
  • Spotlights. For most recessed spotlights, the boxing should be at least 8 to 10 cm deep, according to Slimster. Make sure there is ventilation around the spots.
  • Extractor hood. Choose heat-resistant material, such as MDF or gypsum fibreboard.
  • Gas pipe. If you are unsure whether a gas pipe may be boxed in, ask a certified installer first. Keep couplings accessible in any case.
  • Bathroom and toilet. Use moisture-resistant plasterboard. More about boxing in a concealed cistern in Renovating a toilet.

Sources: Slimster, Koof laten maken (5 November 2026); Hornbach, access hatch 40 × 40 cm (accessed 22 November 2026, including VAT).

What does having a boxing made cost?

Slimster works through an example: a 3-metre MDF boxing in the living room, with cut-outs for spotlights and a curtain rail. Materials € 120, 6 hours of labour € 330 and a call-out fee of € 45: together € 495 including VAT, or € 165 per metre. A cut-out for a spotlight costs € 15 to € 25, not counting the spot itself. For short sections of around 60 cm to 1 metre, tradespeople often charge a starting rate.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to have a boxing made?

Slimster gives € 75 to € 150 per running metre on average, including materials and labour, and € 70 to € 100 per metre for a simple boxing around pipes. A boxing for an extractor hood costs € 150 to € 250 per metre.

Can I build a boxing with metal studs?

Yes. Metal stud is light and straight and works well for long or tall boxings. You use the same profiles and plasterboards as for a metal stud wall.

Is plasterboard or MDF better for a boxing?

MDF is good for a smooth lacquer finish and spotlights and is cheaper to finish. Plasterboard is cheaper as a material and becomes one with the wall or ceiling once plastered. In a bathroom, choose moisture-resistant plasterboard.

Does a boxing need a hatch?

If there are taps, stop valves or couplings inside the boxing, fit an access hatch. That way you can reach them without breaking open the boxing.

How deep should a boxing with spotlights be?

At least 8 to 10 cm for most recessed spotlights, according to Slimster. Also check the installation depth given by the spotlight manufacturer.
