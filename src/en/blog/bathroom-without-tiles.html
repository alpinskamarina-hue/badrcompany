Béton ciré, microcement and tadelakt: the difference

  • Béton ciré is a thin plaster of cement, mineral fillers, resins and pigments. It has more texture and colour variation. According to Stucstunter it can take light use after 5 to 7 days and is fully cured after 28 days. Homedeal puts the whole job at usually three to five days of work.
  • Microcement is very similar to béton ciré, but looks finer and cleaner. Homedeal calls it slightly better suited to quick renovations, because the layers are thinner. The price is comparable.
  • Tadelakt is a Moroccan lime plaster that is polished with a stone and treated with olive oil soap. It feels softer and is breathable. It takes the most handwork and sharp corners are difficult.

Is it waterproof in the shower?

This is where it often goes wrong. Stucstunter says it plainly: béton ciré is not waterproof by itself. Only with the right protective layer is it suitable for a walk-in shower, and in the shower zone it needs a waterproof build-up underneath. Microcement gets a topcoat, tadelakt only becomes water-repellent through the soap treatment.

  • Have the substrate in the shower made waterproof first, just as under tiles.
  • Ask which sealer or topcoat the installer uses and how often it needs renewing.
  • A poor substrate or wrong application can cause cracks, Homedeal writes about tadelakt. Slimster mentions fibreglass mesh as a way to prevent cracks.

Over existing tiles?

Béton ciré and microcement can go over existing tiles because they are so thin. The grout joints are filled first and a bonding layer or primer is applied (Stucstunter, Homedeal). That saves demolition. Tadelakt prefers a flat mineral substrate: Homedeal mentions tiles only with heavy preparation, Bobex advises against it. If the floor underneath is uneven, it needs levelling first; see Floor levelling.

Frequently asked questions

What does béton ciré cost per m² in a bathroom?

According to Homedeal € 120 to € 200 per m² for having it applied in a bathroom or other wet room. Stucstunter charges from € 116.95 per m² excluding VAT, including application and protective layer.

Is béton ciré waterproof?

Not by itself. Béton ciré becomes waterproof through the sealer or PU lacquer on top. In a walk-in shower it also needs a waterproof build-up underneath, Stucstunter writes.

What is the difference between béton ciré and microcement?

They are very similar: both a thin cement-based layer with a protective coat on top. Microcement is usually slightly thinner and looks finer and cleaner, béton ciré has more texture. The price is comparable.

Can béton ciré go over existing tiles?

Yes. The grout joints are filled and a bonding layer or primer is applied. That saves demolition. For tadelakt a mineral substrate is better.

Is a bathroom without tiles cheaper?

Usually not. Seamless costs about € 120 to € 200 per m² in a bathroom, tiling with ordinary ceramic tiles is often less. You sometimes save on demolition, though, because béton ciré and microcement can go over old tiles.
