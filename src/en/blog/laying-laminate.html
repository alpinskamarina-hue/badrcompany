Short answer: you can lay laminate yourself (in Dutch: laminaat leggen) in about a day per room. Leave the packs in the room for at least 48 hours first, make the floor clean, dry and flat, lay an underlay and click the planks together as a floating floor with 8 to 10 mm of space along the walls. Skirting boards cover that gap. Having it laid usually costs € 7 to € 15 per m² in labour according to Trustoo, or € 25 to € 55 per m² including laminate, underlay and skirting.

What do you need?

Laying a click floor takes few tools. Gamma and Praxis, two Dutch DIY chains, list these in their step-by-step guides:

  • To measure and mark: tape measure, pencil, try square or sliding bevel.
  • To cut: a laminate cutter for straight cuts and a jigsaw with a laminate blade for cut-outs around pipes and door frames.
  • To lay: spacers, a tapping block, a pull bar and a (rubber) mallet. A flooring kit usually contains the spacers, tapping block and pull bar together.
  • For yourself: knee pads. Gamma also mentions ear protection and safety glasses.

Buy a little more laminate than the number of m². Trustoo works with about 10% cutting waste. According to Praxis there are on average 4 to 9 planks per m², depending on the size.

Laying laminate in 7 steps

  1. Let it acclimatise. Leave the closed packs in the room where the floor will go for at least 48 hours (Gamma, Praxis, Hubo). Praxis: no colder than 18 degrees. Gamma advises placing the packs at least 50 cm from the wall.
  2. Prepare the subfloor. Take out the old floor covering and remove dust and loose debris. The floor must be dry and flat. If there are height differences of more than 4 mm per m², you need to level it first (Praxis). How that works is explained in Floor levelling.
  3. Lay the underlay. Choose the underlay on four points: moisture resistance, levelling ability, thermal insulation and sound reduction (Gamma). Lay the strips tightly side by side and tape the seams (Hubo). If you live in an apartment, first ask the owners' association (VvE) which sound requirement applies.
  4. Lay the first row. Start in the corner furthest from the door (Hubo). Place 8 to 10 mm spacers between the wall and the laminate (Praxis). The first piece of a row must be at least 30 cm long (Gamma, Praxis).
  5. Keep clicking. Place the new plank at an angle against the previous one, click it down and tap the end joint closed with the tapping block. Start the next row with the offcut from the previous one, if it is at least 30 cm (Hubo). That way the joints are staggered automatically.
  6. Pipes and door frames. Drill or cut a recess and leave space around the pipe here too. Gamma mentions 1 cm of play at door frames and pipes. Trim the bottom of the door frame so the laminate slides underneath.
  7. Last row and skirting. Cut the last row to width, again with 8 to 10 mm of space to the wall. Pull the last plank into place with the pull bar. Remove the spacers and fix the skirting to the wall, not to the floor.

Which direction should you lay laminate?

The usual rule: lay the planks in the direction of the most light, usually the largest window. Then the joints are least visible. Your Floor gives a few more rules of thumb:

  • Lay parallel to the most-used door.
  • A wide room looks bigger with planks running lengthwise, a narrow long room with planks running across.
  • If the floor runs through several rooms, lengthwise is usually the best choice.
  • If you lay over an old wooden floor, do not lay in the same direction as the boards underneath.

Common mistakes

  • Too little space along the wall. Laminate moves with temperature and humidity. Without an 8 to 10 mm gap it can jam. Also watch door frames, pipes and the transition to another room.
  • Not letting it acclimatise. Packs straight from a cold van or shed need 48 hours at room temperature first.
  • A floor that is too uneven. An underlay absorbs small unevenness, but above 4 mm per m² you should level (Praxis). Otherwise the floor flexes and the click joints can fail.
  • Pieces too short and joints in line. Keep every piece at least 30 cm long and stagger the end joints.
  • Nailing skirting to the floor. A floating floor must be able to move freely. Fix the skirting to the wall.

Frequently asked questions

What does laying laminate cost per m²?

According to Trustoo, the laying alone usually costs € 7 to € 15 per m². With laminate, underlay and skirting included it is € 25 to € 55 per m². Badr Company charges € 12 per m² excl. VAT for laying.

Can I lay laminate myself?

Yes. A click floor is a job many people do themselves. Praxis estimates about a day. Pay particular attention to a flat subfloor, 48 hours of acclimatising and 8 to 10 mm of space along the walls.

How much gap do you leave between laminate and the wall?

8 to 10 mm, according to Praxis and Hubo. The floor needs that space to move. The skirting covers the gap.

How long does laminate need to acclimatise?

At least 48 hours, in the closed packs, in the room where the floor will go. Praxis gives a temperature of at least 18 degrees.

Which direction do you lay laminate?

Usually in the direction of the light from the largest window. Then the joints are least visible. In a narrow, long room, running across can look better.

How much extra laminate should I buy?

Allow about 10% extra for cutting waste, says Trustoo. In a room with many corners or a herringbone pattern it is more.
