Three ways to renovate a ceiling

  • Plastering. The plasterer applies a thin coat of gypsum plaster to the existing ceiling and smooths it. The result is a flat ceiling, ready for paint.
  • Spraying (spack). A sprayer applies a coat of spray plaster with a machine. That gives a fine grain or texture, usually in white.
  • Plasterboard. A new ceiling of plasterboard is fixed to timber battens or metal profiles below the old ceiling. The joints are then finished and the ceiling is plastered or sprayed.

Which finish suits your ceiling?

  • Choose plastering if the ceiling is sound and fairly flat, and you want a smooth, modern ceiling. We repair small cracks and holes first.
  • Choose spraying if you like a texture or want the lowest price per m², and the substrate is intact. A texture is harder to make smooth later: the ceiling then has to be sanded or plastered again first.
  • Choose plasterboard if the old ceiling is loose or badly cracked, if there is wooden panelling or an old suspended ceiling, or if you want to hide pipes, spotlights or insulation.

Wooden panelling or a poor ceiling: remove it or cover it?

Wooden ceiling panelling (schrootjes) can be removed, or left in place with a plasterboard ceiling fitted underneath. Leaving it saves demolition, dust and disposal, but the ceiling comes down a little. If we remove the panelling, we usually screw the plasterboard straight onto the existing joists or battens.

An old reed or plaster ceiling that has come loose cannot be plastered or sprayed: the new coat would be just as loose. Here too, a new plasterboard ceiling is the answer. You can then simply plaster over the plasterboard once the joints are finished and the ceiling is primed. Read more in Priming before plastering.

How a ceiling is plastered

  1. Masking. Floor, frames and furniture are covered with plastic sheeting and protective runner.
  2. Preparation. Loose parts come off, cracks and holes are filled. Old distemper or paint layers that are peeling are removed.
  3. Priming. The primer makes the plaster bond well and stops it drying too fast.
  4. Plastering. The plasterer applies the plaster, levels it and smooths it.
  5. Drying. Ventilate well and don't turn the heating up high. Allow about one day per millimetre of thickness.
  6. Painting. Once the ceiling is dry it is painted white. A new ceiling gets a primer first.

Frequently asked questions

What does it cost to have a ceiling plastered?

Price guides give € 15 to € 25 per m² for smooth plastering. At Badr Company plastering a ceiling costs € 18 per m² excl. VAT, or € 19.62 with 9% VAT in a home older than 2 years. Materials come on top.

Which is cheaper, plastering or spraying?

Spraying is usually cheaper per m². Price guides give about € 6 to € 16 per m² for a spack spray finish, against € 15 to € 25 for smooth plastering. A spray finish does give a texture instead of a smooth ceiling.

Can you plaster over plasterboard?

Yes. First the joints and screw holes are finished, then the ceiling is primed and plastered. That gives one smooth surface without visible joints.

Is spraying a ceiling difficult?

For a DIYer, yes. You need a spray machine, the whole room has to be masked and the finish must be applied evenly in one go. That is why most people have a sprayer do it.

How long does a plastered ceiling take to dry?

As a rule of thumb, about one day per millimetre of thickness. Ventilate well and don't turn the heating up high, so it dries evenly and doesn't crack. Painting can only start once the ceiling is completely dry.
