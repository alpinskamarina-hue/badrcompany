Three finish levels

How far you finish plasterboard depends on what goes on top. The thinner the final layer, the smoother the substrate has to be.

LevelWhat is doneSuitable for
C, basicJoints filled and taped with mesh, boards not sandedVinyl wallpaper, spray plaster, spack finish
B, wallpaper-readyJoints filled with mesh tape, lightly sandedThick wallpaper, spack finish
A, smoothAll joints and imperfections filled, sanded and primedPaint, thin wallpaper

Source: Stuc-Gigant, Gipsplaten afwerken (updated 5 October 2026).

For a truly smooth, paint-ready wall or ceiling we skim-plaster the whole board. Then you won't see joints or screw heads, even in raking light.

How a finished joint is built up

Standard plasterboard has tapered long edges (AK). Two boards side by side form a shallow channel that takes the reinforcement and the filler.

Finishing plasterboard in 6 steps

  1. Check. The boards must be flat and dry and the screws properly countersunk. Finish any wet work in the room first: moisture can cause hairline cracks in the joints later.
  2. Mesh tape. Apply self-adhesive mesh tape along the full length of the joints. This helps prevent cracks.
  3. Filling. Fill the joints and screw holes with joint filler, using a filling knife of about 15 cm or a trowel. Remove excess straight away. Stuc-Gigant allows at least 4 hours of drying between coats and fills joints at least three times.
  4. Finisher and sanding. Apply a thin coat of joint finisher over joints and screw heads. Let everything dry well and sand smooth with a sanding block. Remove the dust with a brush.
  5. Priming. Prime the boards with a deep primer. It evens out the suction of board and joint, so the joint doesn't show through after painting. Let the primer dry for 12 to 24 hours.
  6. Final layer. Then wallpaper, paint or a spray finish can go on, or we skim the whole wall paint-ready.

Sources: Gyproc, Voegen van wanden en plafonds (updated 20 September 2022); Gamma, Gipsplaten afwerken (consulted 16 November 2026; 24 hours drying after priming); Stuc-Gigant, Gipsplaten afwerken (updated 5 October 2026; at least 4 hours between coats, 12 hours after priming).

Finishing without plastering?

That is possible. With filled joints and screw holes, finisher and primer you get a wall that is suitable for wallpaper or a spray finish. Painting is also possible, but in raking light you will more easily see the difference between the paper face of the board and the filled joints. The smoother the result has to be, the more filling work is needed.

For ceilings made of plasterboard with rounded edges (RK), Gamma says you can also leave the joints open. You then run a thin bead of acrylic sealant into the joint and smooth it. If you want a sleek, seamless ceiling, plastering is the better choice. Read more in Ceiling: plaster, spray finish or plasterboard?

Frequently asked questions

How do you make plasterboard smooth?

Fill joints and screw holes in a few thin coats, apply a coat of finisher and sand everything smooth. For a completely smooth result, the whole board is filled or skim-plastered and then primed.

Is mesh tape necessary?

For boards with tapered edges (AK), yes: Gyproc prescribes reinforcement tape along the full length of the joint. With rounded-edge ceiling boards (RK) you can also leave the joint open and seal it with acrylic sealant.

Can I finish plasterboard without plastering?

Yes. With filled joints, finisher and primer the board is ready for wallpaper or a spray finish. For paint or thin wallpaper the surface needs to be smoother; then filling or skim-plastering the whole board is better.

Does plasterboard need priming?

Yes. A deep primer evens out the suction of board and joint, so the joints don't show through and paint or plaster bonds well. Let the primer dry for 12 to 24 hours.

Which plasterboard thickness do I need?

For walls 12.5 mm is the most used and available in the most sizes. Gyproc also supplies its standard board in 6, 9.5 and 15 mm. For your wall or ceiling, follow the manufacturer's system instructions.
