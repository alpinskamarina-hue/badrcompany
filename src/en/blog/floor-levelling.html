When does a floor need levelling?

A new floor is only as flat as the surface underneath it. Every unevenness shows through after a few weeks, especially with thin PVC flooring. Levelling is needed if you see any of the following:

  • Dips and height differences. Lay a straight 2-metre rule on the floor. If light shows underneath or the rule rocks, the floor is not flat enough.
  • Cracks and damage in the screed.
  • Adhesive residue from an old floor or carpet.
  • A rough or sandy surface. If you can scratch grit loose with a screwdriver, the subfloor needs to be strengthened first.
  • A soft floor covering. Bostik, a manufacturer of levelling compound and floor adhesive, advises always levelling the screed under soft floors such as glued PVC and vinyl.

With laminate and click PVC, the underlay absorbs small irregularities. Larger height differences still have to go. Check the manufacturer's installation guide for how many millimetres of deviation are allowed.

A cement screed needs about 28 days to cure fully. Plan for that time before the new floor can go on top.

How floor levelling works, step by step

  1. Check the subfloor. Measure moisture, do a scratch test, remove loose parts and adhesive residue. The screed must be dry enough: a cement screed at most 2.0 CM% (1.8 CM% with underfloor heating), an anhydrite screed at most 0.5 CM% (0.3 CM% with underfloor heating).
  2. Sand and vacuum. Dust and grease stop the compound from bonding.
  3. Apply primer. Primer stops the subfloor from absorbing water, so the compound does not dry too fast or come loose. A highly absorbent floor gets a special bonding primer.
  1. Tape the edges. Edge strip goes along the walls, so the compound cannot run away and the floor can move.
  2. Mix the compound. Exactly as the product sheet says: for example 6 litres of water per 25 kg bag. Too much water makes the layer weak.
  3. Pour and spread. The compound is poured in strips and spread with a trowel. A spiked roller removes the air bubbles. This has to be quick: the compound stays workable for about 25 minutes.
  4. Let it dry. Keep windows closed, avoid draughts and direct sunlight.

Need a second layer and the first one is already dry? Prime again first.

How long does levelling compound take to dry?

Drying time depends on the product, the layer thickness and the temperature. For a typical cement-based levelling compound:

  • Conditions: floor warmer than 15 °C, air warmer than 18 °C, humidity below 75%.
Watch outIn a cold room in winter it takes longer. Never lay a floor on compound that is still releasing moisture: the adhesive will not bond well and PVC can start to bulge.

What makes levelling more expensive?

  • A thick layer, or two layers instead of one.
  • Old adhesive, tiles or carpet that have to be removed first.
  • A sandy or damp screed that needs treatment first.
  • A small floor area: fixed costs weigh more heavily.

Level it yourself or hire a pro?

DIY levelling is possible in a small room with a good, dry subfloor. You save on labour. The risk lies in three things:

  • Speed. The compound stays workable for about 25 minutes. In a 30 m² living room you have to mix and pour six bags back to back. That is almost impossible on your own.
  • Water ratio. Too much water gives a weak layer with hairline cracks; too little and it will not flow flat.
  • Subfloor. You cannot always spot a damp or sandy screed with the naked eye. Mistakes only show once the new floor is down.

A failed layer has to be sanded off or poured again, which costs more than having it done right the first time. So hire a pro for large rooms, for height differences of more than a few millimetres and under a glued PVC floor.

Levelling before a PVC floor

PVC is thin and soft, so every unevenness becomes visible. For a glued PVC floor:

  • levelling is always needed, even if the floor looks flat;
  • the compound layer must be completely dry before the adhesive goes on;
  • with underfloor heating, stricter moisture limits apply: 1.8 CM% for cement, 0.3 CM% for anhydrite.

Frequently asked questions

How many mm can you level in one go?

Standard levelling compound goes up to about 10 mm. Special compound can do 30–40 mm. If the difference is bigger, you need a new screed.

Do you always need to prime before levelling?

Yes. Without primer the subfloor sucks the water out of the compound. The layer then dries too fast, cracks and bonds poorly.

Can you level over tiles?

Yes, if the tiles are firmly fixed. Loose tiles must be removed first. On glazed tiles you use a primer for non-absorbent surfaces.

When can I lay PVC after levelling?

For a layer up to 3 mm, after about 24 hours. For a thicker layer, after 48–72 hours. Always check the product sheet of the compound.

Is levelling needed under laminate?

Not always. The underlay absorbs small irregularities. Height differences larger than the laminate manufacturer allows must be removed.
