Types of cracks and their causes

Not every crack is the same. The shape and the location often tell you where it comes from.

Type of crackWhat does it look like?Most common cause
Shrinkage crack, hairline cracksThin lines, often in a web, on a flat wall or ceilingPlaster and wall lose moisture and shrink; plaster that dries too fast
Joint crackA straight line at a transition: wall and ceiling, brick and plasterboard, or a joint between boardsTwo materials that move differently
Settlement crackDiagonal or stepped, often from the corner of a door or windowSettling or subsidence of the foundation or the structure
Horizontal crackA long, level line across the wallPressure on the wall; can point to a structural problem

Sources: Stucstunter, Scheuren in stucwerk (updated 8 October 2026); MAX Vandaag, Scheuren in uw muur (7 August 2026); Homedeal, Stucwerk repareren (24 November 2025).

Stucstunter names drying too fast as the most common cause of cracks in plaster. Moisture can also make plaster crack or come loose, for example after a leak or in a poorly ventilated room.

How serious is the crack?

Three things tell you the most:

  • Width. Homedeal calls hairline cracks narrower than 2 mm superficial and usually harmless. Cracks wider than 2 mm often point to subsidence, structural movement or foundation problems.
  • Direction. Horizontal cracks deserve extra attention. Diagonal or stepped cracks along the mortar joints often occur with subsidence.
  • Growth. Is the crack getting longer or wider? Then something is still moving. Draw a pencil mark at both ends and write the date next to it. After a few weeks you will see whether it grows.

Do doors or windows suddenly stick, does the floor slope or does the wall bulge? Call in a structural engineer. If in doubt, use the Scheurenwijzer (crack guide) of the Dutch homeowners' association Vereniging Eigen Huis.

Cracks in new plaster

In a new-build home or after a renovation, a few shrinkage cracks in the first year are almost unavoidable, Stucstunter writes. The house is still drying out. So have them repaired only once everything is dry, and don't paint or wallpaper too early. How long new plaster needs to dry is explained in Priming plaster walls: how long must new plaster dry?

After plastering, raise the heating gently: about 1 °C per day or every other day, up to 20 °C at most, and ventilate well. Drying too fast with a heater or dehumidifier is a well-known cause of cracks.

Repairing a crack: how it works

You repair a small shrinkage crack in a wall like this, following the step-by-step guide from the Dutch DIY store Praxis:

  1. Remove loose bits. Open the crack up slightly with a screwdriver to make room for the filler.
  2. Clean. Brush the dust away. If the wall is greasy, as in a kitchen, degrease it first.
  3. Fill. Apply a crack filler or acrylic sealant into the crack, holding the gun at about 45 degrees. Smooth it straight away with a wet filling knife.
  4. Finish. Let it dry properly, sand lightly and paint over the spot with the same wall paint as the rest of the wall.

Source: Praxis, step-by-step guide Scheuren in de muur repareren.

A joint crack, for example between ceiling and wall or on a joint between plasterboards, often comes back if you only fill it. That is why a plasterer usually works with mesh: the crack is opened up, mesh tape goes over it and the spot is then given a new thin coat of plaster. For plasterboard, see Finishing plasterboard.

Are there many cracks, does the plaster sound hollow or is it coming loose? Then it is often quicker and neater to give the whole wall a new thin coat than to fill everything spot by spot.

Preventing cracks

  • Let plaster dry gently. No heater on full and no dehumidifier right after plastering. Do ventilate well.
  • Mesh at transitions. Have mesh applied where brick meets plasterboard or timber before plastering.
  • Cause first. Never repair before a leak or damp problem has really been solved.
  • Patience with new builds. Have the small cracks repaired in one go after the first year, then paint.

Frequently asked questions

Are cracks in plaster serious?

Usually not. Most cracks are shrinkage cracks that appear as the wall and plaster lose moisture. Do watch out for cracks wider than 2 mm, horizontal or stepped cracks, or cracks that grow. Have those assessed first.

How do I repair hairline cracks in plaster?

Open the crack slightly, remove dust and loose bits, fill with a crack filler or acrylic sealant and smooth it with a wet filling knife. Let it dry, sand lightly and paint over the spot.

Why does a crack keep coming back?

Because something is still moving, for example at a transition between two materials or due to settlement. On a joint, mesh under a new thin coat of plaster works better than filling alone. If a crack keeps returning because of settlement, have the cause investigated first.

Are cracks in a new-build home normal?

Yes, a few shrinkage cracks in the first year are almost unavoidable because the house is still drying out. Have them repaired once everything is dry.
