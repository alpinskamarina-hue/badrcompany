What is priming?

Primer (in Dutch: voorstrijk) is a thin, watery product you roll or brush onto a wall. It controls the suction of the surface. A wall that absorbs too much water pulls the water out of the plaster or paint. That then dries too fast, cracks or bonds poorly. On a smooth, sealed wall a special primer does the opposite: it makes the surface rough so the plaster can grip it.

When do you need to prime?

Not every wall needs priming. This is how to test it, according to the Alabastine product sheet: wet the wall with a sponge or plant sprayer. If the water is almost completely absorbed, priming is needed. If the water stays on the surface as drops, the wall does not absorb and an ordinary primer is not needed.

  • Always after plastering, before paint or wallpaper. New plaster is very absorbent.
  • Before plastering on very or unevenly absorbent walls, such as sand-lime brick, aerated concrete or old plaster.
  • Before plastering on smooth walls, such as concrete, tiles or old paint. These need a bonding primer.
  • On a powdery wall: if white dust stays on your finger, the primer binds the loose powder.

How long must new plaster dry?

Only prime new plaster once it is completely dry. According to Homedeal, plaster dries about 1 mm per day. There is no exact drying time: thickness, temperature, humidity and ventilation all play a part. Ideal is 20–22 °C with a window opened regularly.

Test whether the wall is dry. Tape a piece of plastic film to the wall. If there is no condensation behind it after 24 hours, the wall is dry enough. Dark patches still showing on the plaster mean it still holds moisture.

Priming in 5 steps

  1. Prepare the wall. Fill holes and cracks, sand away bumps and make the wall dust-free.
  2. Do the water test. Then you know whether and how much to dilute.
  3. Mix the primer. Dilute according to the label, for example 1 part primer to 3 parts water. Too much water makes the primer less effective.
  4. Apply. With a block brush or a roller, wet-in-wet and from top to bottom. Make sure the layer is even everywhere, without puddles.
  5. Let it dry. A fast-drying primer can be painted over after about 3 hours. Other types need 15 hours. Plaster primer dries in about 6 hours at 23 °C. Don't wait too long before plastering, or dust settles on it again.

Common priming mistakes

  • Priming too early. On wet plaster you seal the moisture in. Paint then peels later.
  • Diluting too much. According to Knauf, an over-diluted primer loses its effect.
  • On a dusty wall. Primer binds a little dust, but not a layer of sanding dust. Vacuum or wipe the wall first.
  • The wrong primer on a smooth wall. On concrete or tiles, plaster only bonds with a bonding primer.
  • Working below 10 °C. Many primers must not be applied then.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need to prime before plastering?

Only if the wall is very or unevenly absorbent, or smooth and sealed. Do the water test: if water soaks straight in, use a diluted plaster primer. On concrete, tiles or old paint, use a bonding primer.

Does new plaster need priming before painting?

Yes. New plaster is very absorbent, so paint dries patchy and bonds poorly. Prime with a 1 : 3 primer once the plaster is completely dry.

How long to wait between primer and paint?

It depends on the product. A fast-drying primer can be painted over after about 3 hours, an ordinary primer after about 15 hours. Check the label.

What is the difference between undercoat and primer?

Primer controls the suction of the wall and binds dust, but does not cover. An undercoat is a first coat of paint that also evens out the colour. On new plaster you prime first and then paint with wall paint.

Do you need to prime plasterboard?

Yes, before paint or wallpaper. Plasterboard and the filled joints absorb differently. A primer makes the paint dry evenly everywhere.
