What is spachtelputz?

Spachtelputz is a ready-to-use decorative plaster from the bucket. It consists of synthetic resin as a binder with hard grains in it. The grains set the texture: the layer is exactly as thick as one grain. Common grain sizes are 1 mm, 1.2 mm and 1.5 mm. On walls 1.5 mm is used most.

Spachtelputz is the best-known type of decorative plaster (sierpleister) in the Netherlands. Other types are granol, rustiekputz and krabputz. You can recognise spachtelputz by its fine, even grain with small swirling grooves from the rubbing.

Pros and cons of spachtelputz

ProsCons
Scratch- and impact-resistant, good for a hallway or children's roomA repair often stays visible
The colour is in the material, so a scratch shows lessThe rough grain holds dust and dirt
Small irregularities in the wall disappearPainting over it takes at least 30% more paint than a smooth wall
Finished straight away: no painting neededYou cannot wallpaper over the grain without smoothing it first
Can be repainted laterIn a new-build home that is still settling, small cracks show quickly

Spachtelputz suits a wall that takes a lot of wear, and people who want a finished wall quickly without a painter. It suits a sleek, modern interior less, or if you often change colour or wallpaper. Then paint-ready plastering is the better choice.

What makes spachtelputz more expensive?

  • A ceiling instead of a wall. Working overhead takes more time.
  • A poor surface. Cracks, loose layers or old spachtelputz must be removed or levelled first.
  • A special colour. Spachtelputz in a RAL or NCS colour costs more than white.
  • A small area. Below € 250 the minimum charge applies.

How spachtelputz is applied

  1. Check the surface. According to the product sheet the wall must be flat, sound, clean, dry and dust-free. Cracks are repaired first and loose layers come off. The 1.5 mm grain hides small irregularities, but not bumps or holes.
  2. Tape and cover. The floor, frames and sockets are covered.
  3. Prime. A primer (putzgrond) stops the suction of the wall and makes the spachtelputz bond. Without primer the layer dries too fast and can come loose later.
  4. Mix the buckets. Buckets from different production batches can differ slightly in colour. That is why the plasterer mixes them together first.
  5. Apply. The spachtelputz goes on the wall with a stainless steel trowel, exactly as thick as the grain.
  1. Rub up. With a plastic float in small circles. A wall is done in one go from corner to corner, otherwise you will later see where the plasterer stopped.
  2. Let it dry. Do not apply below 8 °C. After a few days the layer is fully cured.
NoteSpachtelputz can also be sprayed with a pump. That is faster on large surfaces but needs more masking. For a living room most plasterers choose the trowel.

Old spachtelputz: keep, paint, smooth over or remove?

Many Dutch homes from the 1970s and 1980s have spachtelputz on the walls. If you buy such a home, you have four options:

OptionResultChoose this if
CleanGrain stays, colour looks fresherthe layer is firm and only dirty. Dirt deep in the grain rarely comes out completely.
PaintGrain stays, new colouryou don't mind the texture and want it done quickly, without the drying time of a plaster layer
Smooth overFlat wall, ready for paint or wallpaperthe spachtelputz is firmly attached and you want a modern, smooth wall
RemoveBack to the bare wallthe layer comes loose or bulges, or a new tiled wall or finish has to go on

If the old spachtelputz is firmly attached, smoothing it over is almost always smarter than removing it. Removing creates a lot of dust and extra hours, and afterwards the wall still has to be plastered.

Painting spachtelputz

You can paint spachtelputz with ordinary wall paint. Expect at least 30% more paint than on a smooth wall, because the paint has to get between the grains. This is how to do it:

  1. Make the wall free of dust and grease.
  2. Use a roller with a long pile, so the paint gets into the grooves.
  3. Apply two coats. For a big change of colour a third coat is sometimes needed.

Painting is the quickest and cheapest way to refresh an old spachtelputz wall. You do keep the grain and the dust that clings to it. If you want it done for you, see painting.

Repairing spachtelputz: can it be invisible?

A hole from a wall plug or a damaged spot is repaired with a little spachtelputz of the same grain. Apply it with a filling knife and rub it in small circles with your finger or a small float, like the rest of the wall. If the wall is painted, touch up the spot afterwards.

A repair is almost never completely invisible: the texture and colour of old and new differ slightly. If you don't want to see anything, paint the whole wall from corner to corner. With many holes or loose patches, smoothing the wall over is cheaper than patching again and again.

Making spachtelputz smooth

If you want to get rid of the grain without demolition, the plasterer plasters over it. That only works if the spachtelputz is firmly attached. Knock on the wall: if it sounds hollow or grit comes loose, that part has to come off first.

  1. Remove loose parts and clean the wall.
  2. Apply a bonding primer. Gypsum only bonds to the smooth resin with a special primer.
  3. Fill the grain. A first layer of plaster fills the texture.
  4. Apply the finishing coat. Wallpaper-ready or paint-ready, depending on what goes on next.

Smoothing costs more than ordinary plastering, because a bonding layer and a filling layer are added. How much exactly depends on the grain and the condition of the wall. That is why we give a fixed price after a free visit. The new layer must be fully dry before you paint or wallpaper.

Removing spachtelputz

Removing is only needed if the layer comes loose or the wall has to go back to the bare surface. There are three ways:

  • Scraping with a filling knife or scraper. Works well on a layer that is already partly loose.
  • Sanding with a sander with dust extraction. Fast, but with a lot of dust and sandpaper.
  • Steaming with a steamer, so the layer softens and can be scraped off. Be careful on a gypsum wall: the moisture can damage it.

Plasterers quote about € 15 per m² for removal. After that the wall often has to be plastered again. If you do it yourself, always wear a dust mask and safety glasses, and cover the room well.

Frequently asked questions

Can you wallpaper over spachtelputz?

Not directly. Wallpaper sticks poorly to the grain and the texture shows through. Have the wall smoothed first with a bonding primer and a wallpaper-ready layer, then you can wallpaper.

Do you need to prime before spachtelputz?

Yes. A primer (putzgrond) stops the suction of the wall and makes it bond. On a very absorbent or weak surface a special primer is needed.

Can I apply spachtelputz with a roller?

According to the product sheet spachtelputz is applied with a stainless steel trowel and rubbed up with a plastic float, or sprayed with a pump. A roller is meant for the primer and for paint over the spachtelputz.

Can I apply spachtelputz myself?

On a small, flat wall you can. The hard part is an even texture: you have to finish the wall in one go and mix the buckets beforehand. On a large wall or ceiling the joins show quickly.

How do you clean spachtelputz?

With a soft brush and lukewarm water. Stubborn stains need a special cleaner for decorative plaster. Dirt deep in the grain rarely comes out completely. Then painting helps.
